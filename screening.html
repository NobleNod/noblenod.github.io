Screening

I review your form submission. This is where I decide if we should talk.

📋 What happens at this stage

You submitted the form. I have your details. Now I'm looking at your resume and your project description to decide if you're worth a conversation.

This isn't a pass/fail gate based on your college name or GPA. I'm looking for signals that you've actually built something and can think.

Most submissions get a response within 48 hours. If I need more information, I'll email you. If you're a fit, I'll schedule a call. If you're not a fit for the current role, I'll tell you why and keep you on file for future opportunities.

✓ What I look for

  • 1

    Honesty over polish

    I don't care if your project is small. I care if you can explain what you built, what broke, and what you learned. Real answers beat impressive-sounding nonsense.

  • 2

    A real project

    Something you actually built. Not a tutorial you followed. Not code you copied. Something where you made decisions and solved problems.

  • 3

    Clear communication

    Can you explain what you did in simple terms? Can you describe what was hard without resorting to buzzwords? This matters more than fancy vocabulary.

  • 4

    Fit for the role

    Right now I need 2024/2025 graduates who are Chennai-based or willing to relocate. If that's not you, you might still be a fit for future opportunities.

✗ What gets you rejected

  • ✗

    AI-generated answers

    I can spot them instantly. "Scalable solutions," "overcame challenges," "rewarding experience" in perfectly structured paragraphs. If ChatGPT wrote it, I'll know. I'll give you one chance to rewrite. Don't waste it.

  • ✗

    No real project

    If your project description is just a list of technologies with no explanation of what you actually built or what was hard, there's nothing for me to evaluate.

  • ✗

    Ghosting

    If I email you asking for clarification and you disappear, I move on. I respond to everyone. I expect the same respect.

  • ✗

    Lying

    About your experience, your location, your graduation date. If I catch it now or later, you're done. No second chances for dishonesty.

If I ask you to rewrite your answer

It means I saw potential but your submission looked AI-generated or too vague. This is your second chance. Tell me what you actually built. What broke. What confused you. What you figured out. Keep it short and real. That's all I need.

If I schedule a call

You passed the screening. You'll get an email with available time slots. Pick one. Show up. Be ready to talk about your project in detail and solve a problem or two live. See Stage 3 for how to prepare.

If you're not a fit for the current role

I'll tell you why. Maybe you're still in college. Maybe you're too experienced for a fresher role. Maybe you're not willing to relocate. Whatever the reason, I'll be direct. And I'll keep your info for future opportunities that might be a better match.
